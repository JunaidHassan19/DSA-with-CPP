GREEDY ALGORITHMS - QUICK NOTES

1) What is a Greedy Algorithm?
A greedy algorithm is a method where, at each step, we choose the option that looks best at that moment (locally optimal choice).
We do not go back and change previous choices.

2) Core Idea
- Make the best immediate decision.
- Hope that these local best decisions combine to form a global best answer.

3) When Greedy Works
Greedy works only for problems that satisfy:
- Greedy Choice Property: A global optimum can be reached by choosing local optimum choices.
- Optimal Substructure: Optimal solution of the whole problem contains optimal solutions to subproblems.

4) General Greedy Steps
1. Understand what must be optimized (minimize or maximize).
2. Decide the greedy criterion (what is the "best" next choice).
3. Sort or prioritize data based on that criterion (if needed).
4. Pick choices one by one while maintaining feasibility.
5. Stop when no valid choice remains.

5) Common Examples
- Activity Selection Problem (select maximum non-overlapping activities)
- Fractional Knapsack (take items by highest value/weight ratio)
- Minimum Number of Coins (for canonical coin systems)
- Job Sequencing with Deadlines
- Huffman Coding
- Kruskal's and Prim's Algorithm (Minimum Spanning Tree)
- Dijkstra's Algorithm (shortest path with non-negative edges)

6) Important Note
Greedy does NOT always work.
Example: Coin change with coins {1, 3, 4} and amount = 6
- Greedy: 4 + 1 + 1 (3 coins)
- Optimal: 3 + 3 (2 coins)

7) Advantages
- Simple to implement
- Fast in many practical cases
- Often gives optimal answer for specific problem classes

8) Disadvantages
- May fail for many problems
- Needs proof of correctness for each problem
- Local best choice can lead to global wrong answer

9) Time Complexity Pattern
Many greedy solutions involve sorting first:
- Sorting: O(n log n)
- Single scan/selection after sort: O(n)
- Total (common case): O(n log n)

10) How to Identify a Greedy Problem in Interviews
- Problem asks for min/max optimization.
- You can define a clear "best next step".
- A choice made now does not destroy possibility of optimal final answer.
- You can justify/prove why local choice leads to global optimum.

11) Short Summary
Greedy algorithms choose the best immediate option at each step.
They are efficient and elegant, but only correct when the problem has greedy-choice property and optimal substructure.
